9. Open problems and research directions

A first list of gaps that stand out when the methods above are put side by side. None of these has yet been checked against the most recent literature.

9.1 Geometry and dynamics are handled by different methods

Methods that handle varying geometry (GINO, GNOT, Transolver, CORAL, MeshGraphNets) are either steady or step the full field autoregressively. Methods with compact latent dynamics (LDNets, LaSDI, Koopman autoencoders, LED) assume a fixed geometry. A natural direction is geometry-conditioned latent dynamics: give an LDNet's reconstruction and dynamics networks a geometry code (e.g. from a signed distance function encoder) and test on unsteady flows past families of shapes under time-varying inflow.

9.2 Data assimilation for encoder-free latent models

LDNets cannot correct their latent state from new measurements. Because the latent state is small, ensemble Kalman or particle filters in latent space, with the reconstruction network as observation operator at sensor locations, should be cheap and could turn LDNets into practical digital twins.

9.3 Error estimates for learned surrogates

Certified reduced basis methods come with a posteriori error bounds; almost no neural surrogate does. When the decoder is differentiable in space (neural fields, LDNets), the PDE residual of the prediction can be computed cheaply and used as an error indicator, possibly calibrated with conformal prediction on held-out solves.

9.4 Uncertainty and chaotic flows

A small deterministic latent ODE cannot represent turbulence, while generative full-field models are expensive. Stochastic or generative dynamics in a compact latent space (latent SDEs, latent diffusion) combined with a neural-field decoder could give cheap ensembles with the right statistics.

9.5 Structure-preserving latent dynamics

Constraining the learned latent dynamics to be stable, dissipative or port-Hamiltonian could make long rollouts under unseen input signals reliable, at some cost in expressiveness for genuinely unstable regimes such as the onset of vortex shedding.

9.6 Fair comparisons across the two traditions

Hardly anyone compares projection ROMs, neural ROMs and neural operators on the same data, with the same budget and against strong solvers. A small, careful benchmark with time-dependent inputs (inflow, actuation, moving boundaries) and classical baselines included would be valuable on its own and would anchor the directions above.
