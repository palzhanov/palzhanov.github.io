Chapter 3

3. Neural reduced-order models and latent dynamics

These methods keep the reduced-order modeling idea of a small latent state but replace the linear basis, the reduced dynamics, or both, with neural networks.

3.1 Nonlinear-manifold ROMs with autoencoders

Replace the linear approximation \(u_h\approx V_N u_N\) with a nonlinear decoder \(u_h\approx g_\theta(z)\), \(z\in\mathbb{R}^n\), learned by a convolutional autoencoder from snapshots. A nonlinear manifold of small dimension can represent transported features that would need many POD modes. The dynamics are still obtained from the governing equations, by projecting the full-order residual onto the tangent space of the manifold (manifold Galerkin) or by minimizing the residual (manifold LSPG): \[ z^{n+1} = \arg\min_z \big\| r_h\big(g_\theta(z);\mu\big)\big\|. \] This is intrusive and needs hyper-reduction to be fast, but it keeps the physics in the time stepping.

Papers
  • K. Lee, K. T. Carlberg, Model reduction of dynamical systems on nonlinear manifolds using deep convolutional autoencoders, J. Comput. Phys., 2020.

3.2 DL-ROM and POD-DL-ROM

DL-ROM, from the Politecnico di Milano group, makes the nonlinear-manifold idea fully non-intrusive. A decoder \(D\) maps a latent code to the full field, an encoder \(E\) maps snapshots to the latent space (used in training only), and a feed-forward network \(\phi\) maps \((t,\mu)\) to the latent code. Schematically the loss is \[ \omega\,\big\|u_h(t;\mu)-D(\phi(t,\mu))\big\|^2 + (1-\omega)\,\big\|E(u_h(t;\mu))-\phi(t,\mu)\big\|^2. \] Online, the model is just \(D\circ\phi\). POD-DL-ROM first compresses the snapshots with (randomized) POD and runs the autoencoder on the POD coefficients, which makes training much cheaper and allows larger problems.

Limitation: time enters as an input coordinate, so there are no actual dynamics and the model cannot respond to a new time-dependent input signal. This is precisely what LDNets address.

Papers
  • S. Fresca, L. Dede', A. Manzoni, A comprehensive deep learning-based approach to reduced order modeling of nonlinear time-dependent parametrized PDEs, J. Sci. Comput., 2021.
  • S. Fresca, A. Manzoni, POD-DL-ROM: enhancing deep learning-based reduced order models for nonlinear parametrized PDEs by proper orthogonal decomposition, Comput. Methods Appl. Mech. Eng., 2022.

3.3 Graph convolutional autoencoder ROMs (GCA-ROM)

Convolutional autoencoders need structured grids. GCA-ROM uses graph convolutions with mesh-based pooling so the encoder and decoder operate directly on unstructured finite element meshes, with a parameter-to-latent network as in DL-ROM. It brings nonlinear-manifold ROMs to the meshes used in practice and connects the ROM community to the graph-network methods of Chapter 5. The mesh is fixed across samples in the main setting. Which test cases are covered, and whether time dependence is treated, is to verify.

Papers
  • F. Pichi, B. Moya, J. S. Hesthaven, A graph convolutional autoencoder approach to model order reduction for parametrized PDEs, J. Comput. Phys., 2024.

3.4 Koopman autoencoders

Koopman theory says that nonlinear dynamics act linearly on a (generally infinite-dimensional) space of observables. A Koopman autoencoder learns a finite set of observables \(z=\varphi(u)\) in which the dynamics are approximately linear, \[ z_{k+1} = K z_k,\qquad u_k \approx \varphi^{-1}(z_k), \] trained with reconstruction, linear-prediction and multi-step losses. Linear latent dynamics are easy to analyze and to use for control and spectral analysis. Systems with continuous spectra and chaotic flows are hard to capture with a fixed linear operator; the paper addresses this partly with an auxiliary network that makes the eigenvalues depend on the state.

Papers
  • B. Lusch, J. N. Kutz, S. L. Brunton, Deep learning for universal linear embeddings of nonlinear dynamics, Nature Communications, 2018.

3.5 Latent space dynamics identification (LaSDI)

LaSDI combines an autoencoder with SINDy: snapshots are encoded to a latent trajectory \(z(t)\), and a sparse, interpretable ODE \(\dot z = \Theta(z)\,\Xi(\mu)\) is identified in the latent space for each training parameter. For a new parameter, the coefficients \(\Xi(\mu)\) are interpolated. The result is an interpretable latent model with genuine dynamics. Its accuracy depends on how well coefficient interpolation works across the parameter space.

Papers
  • W. D. Fries, X. He, Y. Choi, LaSDI: Parametric latent space dynamics identification, Comput. Methods Appl. Mech. Eng., 2022.
Demo notebook · planned

Autoencoder + SINDy on parametrized 1D Burgers; interpolation of coefficients to unseen parameters.

3.6 Learning effective dynamics (LED)

LED uses an autoencoder to obtain a latent state and a recurrent network (LSTM) to advance it in time. Its distinctive idea is multiscale coupling: the model alternates between long, cheap stretches of latent propagation and short bursts of the full solver that bring the trajectory back to the true dynamics. This trades accuracy against cost explicitly and is an early example of the hybrid approaches in Section 5.3.

Papers
  • P. R. Vlachas, G. Arampatzis, C. Uhler, P. Koumoutsakos, Multiscale simulations of complex systems by learning their effective dynamics, Nature Machine Intelligence, 2022.

3.7 Latent Dynamics Networks (LDNets)

LDNets learn a low-dimensional latent state \(s(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N_s}\) whose evolution is driven by time-dependent inputs \(f(t)\), together with a decoder that returns the field at any query point: \[ \begin{aligned} \dot s(t) &= \mathcal{F}_\theta\big(s(t), f(t)\big),\quad s(0)=s_0,\\ u(x,t)&\approx \mathcal{R}_\theta\big(s(t), x\big). \end{aligned} \] Both \(\mathcal{F}_\theta\) (the dynamics network) and \(\mathcal{R}_\theta\) (the reconstruction network) are small fully connected networks, trained end to end by integrating the latent ODE and matching outputs at observed space–time points.

The key difference from Sections 3.1 to 3.6 is that there is no encoder. The latent state is never obtained by compressing a high-dimensional snapshot; it is generated by the learned dynamics from a fixed starting point, and the model discovers whatever latent coordinates best explain the outputs. Consequences:

  • Training data need not live on a common mesh, and outputs can be queried anywhere in the domain.
  • Time-dependent inputs are first-class, which suits control and digital-twin settings.
  • The models are small, and the latent dimension can be very low.

Limitations and open points: the geometry is fixed; without an encoder, a new observed state (a different initial condition or measurements) cannot be absorbed directly; training backpropagates through the time integration; and there is no built-in physics or error estimate. The paper's test cases include advection–diffusion–reaction, cardiac electrophysiology and an unsteady incompressible flow with time-dependent inputs; the exact configurations are to verify.

Related: DINo (Section 4.6) also combines a latent ODE with a neural-field decoder, and is the closest analogue from the machine-learning community. The reconstruction network \(\mathcal{R}_\theta(s,x)\) has the same "code plus query point" structure as a DeepONet (Section 4.1), with the code coming from a dynamical system instead of a branch network.

Papers
  • F. Regazzoni, S. Pagani, M. Salvador, L. Dede', A. Quarteroni, Learning the intrinsic dynamics of spatio-temporal processes through Latent Dynamics Networks, Nature Communications, 2024.
