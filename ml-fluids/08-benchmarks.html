8. Benchmarks and methodology

How methods are compared matters as much as the methods themselves.

8.1 Benchmark suites

PDEBench collects many 1D, 2D and 3D problems, including advection, Burgers, diffusion–reaction, Darcy flow, shallow water and compressible and incompressible Navier–Stokes, with baseline models and forward and inverse tasks. Shared suites made comparisons easier, but they are mostly initial-value or steady problems on simple domains. ROM-style settings with time-dependent inputs, few training simulations and complex geometry are under-represented. More recent fluid-specific benchmarks (FlowBench, CFDBench and others) are listed for a later update.

Papers
  • M. Takamoto et al., PDEBench: An extensive benchmark for scientific machine learning, NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks, 2022.

8.2 Weak baselines and reporting bias

McGreivy and Hakim reviewed machine-learning papers on fluid-related PDEs and found that many reported speedups compare against weak baselines: numerical solvers that are not state of the art, or comparisons not made at equal accuracy. Combined with the tendency to publish only positive results, this leads to overly optimistic conclusions. Their recommendation is to compare against strong solvers at equal accuracy or equal runtime. The same standard should apply when neural ROMs are compared with well-tuned projection ROMs.

Papers
  • N. McGreivy, A. Hakim, Weak baselines and reporting biases lead to overoptimism in machine learning for fluid-related partial differential equations, Nature Machine Intelligence, 2024.
