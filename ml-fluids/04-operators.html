4. Operator learning

Neural operators learn maps between function spaces, for example from a coefficient field or an initial condition to the solution. They are usually trained on many input–output pairs and aim to be independent of the discretization.

4.1 DeepONet

Motivated by a universal approximation theorem for operators, DeepONet writes the output function as a linear combination of learned basis functions of the query point: \[ \mathcal{G}(a)(y) \approx \sum_{k=1}^{p} b_k(a)\,t_k(y) + b_0. \] The branch network takes the input function \(a\) sampled at fixed sensor locations and outputs the coefficients \(b_k\); the trunk network takes the query location \(y\) (space, and possibly time) and outputs the basis functions \(t_k\). Outputs can be queried anywhere, but inputs must be given at the same sensors. Because the output is linear in the trunk basis, DeepONet behaves like a learned linear reduced basis in the output space and faces the same n-width difficulty for transport-dominated problems.

Papers
  • L. Lu, P. Jin, G. Pang, Z. Zhang, G. E. Karniadakis, Learning nonlinear operators via DeepONet based on the universal approximation theorem of operators, Nature Machine Intelligence, 2021.

4.2 Fourier Neural Operator and the neural operator framework

A neural operator stacks layers of the form \[ v_{l+1}(x) = \sigma\Big(W v_l(x) + \int_\Omega \kappa_\phi(x,y)\,v_l(y)\,dy\Big). \] The Fourier Neural Operator parametrizes the kernel integral in Fourier space: transform, multiply the lowest \(k_{\max}\) modes by learned weights, transform back, \[ v_{l+1} = \sigma\big(W v_l + \mathcal{F}^{-1}(R_\phi\cdot \mathcal{F}v_l)\big). \] With the FFT each layer costs \(O(N\log N)\) on a uniform grid. Since the weights live on Fourier modes, the same model can be evaluated on different grids; how well this "zero-shot super-resolution" works in practice depends on how much high-frequency content the solution has. The general framework, universal approximation results and other kernel parametrizations (graph, low-rank, multipole) are in the JMLR paper.

Limitations: uniform grids; spectral truncation struggles with shocks and sharp fronts; aliasing when changing resolution (see Section 4.4); autoregressive rollouts accumulate error.

Papers
  • Z. Li, N. Kovachki, K. Azizzadenesheli, B. Liu, K. Bhattacharya, A. Stuart, A. Anandkumar, Fourier Neural Operator for parametric partial differential equations, ICLR, 2021.
  • N. Kovachki, Z. Li, B. Liu, K. Azizzadenesheli, K. Bhattacharya, A. Stuart, A. Anandkumar, Neural Operator: Learning maps between function spaces, JMLR, 2023.

4.3 Neural operators on general geometries: Geo-FNO and GINO

FNO needs a regular grid. Geo-FNO learns (or is given) a deformation from the irregular physical domain to a latent uniform grid and applies FNO there. GINO encodes a point cloud and a signed distance function of the geometry onto a regular latent grid with a graph neural operator, applies FNO on that grid, and decodes to arbitrary query points with another graph operator. GINO targets large 3D problems such as surface pressure on car geometries for drag prediction. Both focus mainly on steady problems; unsteady flows with varying geometry remain less explored.

Papers
  • Z. Li, D. Z. Huang, B. Liu, A. Anandkumar, Fourier Neural Operator with learned deformations for PDEs on general geometries, JMLR, 2023.
  • Z. Li et al., Geometry-Informed Neural Operator for large-scale 3D PDEs, NeurIPS, 2023.

4.4 Convolutional Neural Operators (CNO)

CNO builds an operator from U-Net-style convolutions while respecting the equivalence between the continuous operator and its discrete implementation: functions are treated as band-limited, and up-sampling, down-sampling and nonlinearities are arranged to avoid aliasing. The paper argues that this makes the learned map a well-defined operator across resolutions and reports gains over FNO and other baselines on a suite of benchmark PDEs. It also reminds us that a well-designed convolutional network is a strong baseline for operator learning on regular grids.

Papers
  • B. Raonić, R. Molinaro, T. De Ryck, T. Rohner, F. Bartolucci, R. Alaifari, S. Mishra, E. de Bézenac, Convolutional Neural Operators for robust and accurate learning of PDEs, NeurIPS, 2023.

4.5 Transformer operators: Galerkin Transformer, GNOT, Transolver

Treat every mesh point as a token and read attention as a learned kernel integral. The Galerkin Transformer drops the softmax and normalizes keys and values instead, giving linear cost in the number of points and an interpretation as a Petrov–Galerkin projection: \[ z = Q\,\frac{K^\top V}{n}. \] GNOT mixes several input functions (geometry, boundary conditions, parameters) through a normalized cross-attention and a geometry-gated mixture of experts. Transolver introduces "physics attention": each point is softly assigned to one of \(M\ll n\) learned slices, attention runs among the \(M\) slice tokens, and results are broadcast back to the points, \[ w = \operatorname{softmax}(\mathrm{Proj}(x)),\qquad z_j = \frac{\sum_i w_{ij}x_i}{\sum_i w_{ij}}. \] These methods handle unstructured meshes and varying geometry. The slice tokens are effectively a learned low-dimensional representation, which suggests an interesting link to reduced bases that has not been explored much.

Papers
  • S. Cao, Choose a Transformer: Fourier or Galerkin, NeurIPS, 2021.
  • Z. Hao et al., GNOT: A General Neural Operator Transformer for operator learning, ICML, 2023.
  • H. Wu, H. Luo, H. Wang, J. Wang, M. Long, Transolver: A fast Transformer solver for PDEs on general geometries, ICML, 2024.

4.6 Neural-field operators: DINo and CORAL

Represent each field by a small code \(z\) that modulates a shared implicit neural representation \(f_\theta(x; z)\), then learn the dynamics or the operator on the codes. DINo obtains codes by auto-decoding (optimizing a code per sample) and integrates a latent ODE on them, giving a model that is continuous in space and time and works from irregularly sampled observations. CORAL uses the same idea with meta-learned codes and learns operators between codes, handling general geometries. These are the ML-community relatives of LDNets: the main differences are that LDNets do not fit codes to observed states and that their latent dynamics are driven by external inputs.

Papers
  • Y. Yin, M. Kirchmeyer, J.-Y. Franceschi, A. Rakotomamonjy, P. Gallinari, Continuous PDE dynamics forecasting with implicit neural representations, ICLR, 2023.
  • L. Serrano et al., Operator learning with neural fields: Tackling PDEs on general geometries, NeurIPS, 2023.
