Chapter 2

2. Fundamental methods

Projection-based reduced-order models are the baseline every learned surrogate should be compared against when the mesh is fixed. They also supply the vocabulary used later: snapshots, reduced bases, offline/online phases, intrusive vs non-intrusive, and the Kolmogorov n-width.

2.1 Proper orthogonal decomposition and Galerkin projection

Collect snapshots \(S=[u_h(t_1;\mu_1),\dots,u_h(t_m;\mu_m)]\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{N}_h\times m}\) and compute the SVD \(S=U\Sigma W^\top\). The first \(N\) left singular vectors form the POD basis \(V_N\), which is optimal in the least-squares sense among all \(N\)-dimensional linear subspaces for the given snapshots. The solution is approximated as \(u_h\approx V_N u_N\), and the reduced equations come from projecting the full-order residual onto the basis: \[ V_N^\top\big( M V_N \dot u_N + \mathcal{N}_h(V_N u_N;\mu) - f_h(t)\big)=0. \] For nonlinear terms, the projected operator still costs \(O(\mathcal{N}_h)\) to evaluate unless one uses hyper-reduction (empirical interpolation, DEIM) to sample the nonlinearity at a few points.

Strengths: simple, interpretable, the reduced model inherits structure from the discretization. Limitations: intrusive (needs the solver's operators); POD-Galerkin can be unstable for convection-dominated and turbulent flows; and a linear subspace needs many modes when features are transported (the Kolmogorov n-width barrier). This last limitation is the main motivation for the nonlinear methods in Chapter 3.

2.2 Reduced basis methods and certified error bounds

The reduced basis method builds the basis from solutions at carefully chosen parameter values. A greedy algorithm repeatedly adds the snapshot at the parameter where an a posteriori error estimator is largest. For coercive problems with an affine parameter dependence the estimator is rigorous: \[ \|u_h(\mu)-V_N u_N(\mu)\|_X \le \frac{\|r_h(V_N u_N(\mu);\mu)\|_{X'}}{\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}(\mu)}, \] where \(r_h\) is the full-order residual and \(\alpha_{\mathrm{LB}}\) a lower bound of the coercivity (or inf-sup) constant. Thanks to an offline/online decomposition, both the reduced solution and its error bound cost nothing that depends on \(\mathcal{N}_h\) online. Geometric parameters are handled by mapping to a reference domain.

This certification is something almost no neural surrogate offers, and it is a recurring theme in Chapter 9.

2.3 Operator inference

Operator inference keeps the structure of a projection ROM but learns the reduced operators from data instead of projecting the solver. If the governing equations have polynomial nonlinearities (e.g. quadratic advection), the reduced model must have the form \[ \dot{\hat u} = \hat A\hat u + \hat H(\hat u\otimes\hat u) + \hat B f(t), \] with \(\hat u = V_N^\top u_h\). The matrices \(\hat A,\hat H,\hat B\) are fitted by a (regularized) least-squares problem on projected snapshots and their time derivatives. It is non-intrusive, cheap to train, handles time-dependent inputs naturally, and remains interpretable. Its limits are those of the linear subspace plus the assumption of known polynomial structure.

2.4 Sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics (SINDy)

SINDy discovers governing equations by sparse regression. Given state measurements \(X\) and derivatives \(\dot X\), build a library \(\Theta(X)\) of candidate terms (polynomials, trigonometric functions, ...) and solve \[ \dot X \approx \Theta(X)\,\Xi, \qquad \Xi \text{ sparse}, \] with sequentially thresholded least squares or another sparsity-promoting method. The result is a short, readable ODE. SINDy was introduced for low-dimensional systems; for fluids it is applied to POD coefficients or to learned latent coordinates (see LaSDI, Section 3.5).

Papers
  • S. L. Brunton, J. L. Proctor, J. N. Kutz, Discovering governing equations from data by sparse identification of nonlinear dynamical systems, PNAS, 2016.

2.5 POD with neural network regression (POD-NN)

The bridge from classical to learned ROMs. Compute a POD basis, then train a neural network to map parameters directly to the reduced coefficients: \[ u_h(\mu) \approx V_N\,\phi_\theta(\mu). \] No equations are projected, so the method is fully non-intrusive and the online cost is one forward pass. Time can be included as an extra input. It inherits the n-width limitation of the linear basis and has no notion of dynamics, so it cannot respond to new time-dependent inputs. Structurally it is a DeepONet (Section 4.1) whose trunk network has been replaced by POD modes.

Papers
  • J. S. Hesthaven, S. Ubbiali, Non-intrusive reduced order modeling of nonlinear problems using neural networks, J. Comput. Phys., 2018.
